# CLIMBERS — PROLOGUE II CANONICALS

Five references. One character, four locations. Prologue II's thirteen plates
are written and every one of them is blocked until these exist.

Save each result into the path given, under the **exact** filename. `npm run
prompts` checks the filesystem for that string, and a file named anything else
is invisible to the tooling.

---

## READ THIS ONCE BEFORE YOU START

**Attach `CLIMBERS_Pulp_SciFi_Illustration_Project_Guide_v2.md` to the
conversation first.** Every prompt below assumes the guide is loaded and does
not restate its rules.

**A canonical is not a plate.** It is a production reference. Its job is to make
the *next* thirteen images consistent, so it shows construction, not mood.

**Aspect ratio: 3:2 landscape** for the locations, **square** for the character
sheet.

**No roster characters inside a location canonical.** Where a location needs a
human for scale, use an anonymous silhouette with no face and no costume
identity — a canonical location carrying a canonical character contaminates
both.

**Sister Silence already exists** and is not in this sheet. She is one of the
ten Oath Lords and her canonical was filed in `references/OATH_LORDS/`; it has
been moved to `references/CHARACTERS/SISTER_SILENCE/` under the filename the
tooling looks for, and her roster block has been rewritten from the actual art.
She wears a worked-gold band across the eyes and heavy gold-and-black
ecclesiastical vestments — nothing like the plain robes I had first written for
her, and the plate that features her has been corrected to match.

**The man with the book gets no canonical, deliberately.** He is defined by
nobody being able to describe him afterward, and a locked reference sheet would
fix a face, which is the one thing he must not have. He is marked `inline` in
the roster and his description travels inside the two prompts he appears in.

## 1 — COLE PORTER

Save as `references/CHARACTERS/COLE/char-cole-canonical.png`

```
Character reference sheet, CLIMBERS pulp sci-fi illustration style. SQUARE, 1:1.

COLE PORTER — municipal grid contractor, Haven City. Human, mid-fifties.

Full figure, standing, neutral pose, three-quarter view, plain neutral
background. This is a production reference, not a scene: no environment, no
story, no drama, no action.

BUILD: twenty-two years of physical work in the shoulders, forearms and hands.
Solid rather than athletic. Weathered face, grey coming into the hair, the
permanent slight squint of a man who works in bad light. He is a tradesman and
must never read as a soldier, an adventurer or a hero.

THE LENS — the single most important detail on this sheet. A DIAGNOSTIC LENS is
mounted over his LEFT eye on a simple municipal head rig: a strap or light frame
around the skull, an adjustable arm, a thick round optic seated over the left
socket only. The right eye is bare and human. The rig is SCUFFED AND REPAIRED —
tape on one joint, a replaced screw, paint worn off the high points. It is
issued equipment worn every day for two decades. It must NOT look military,
elegant, expensive or cybernetic; nothing about it is implanted, it is a tool
strapped to a man's head. Show it INACTIVE on this sheet, dark, with no glow.

THE NOTEBOOK: a small PAPER notebook riding in his chest pocket, worn soft at
the corners, swollen with use, a PENCIL clipped beside it. Both must be clearly
visible in the pocket.

WARDROBE: heavy municipal work coat over practical layers, in dull municipal
colours — faded ochre, grey-green, oiled brown. A tool belt or kit strap, a
shoulder LAMP on a clip, gloves tucked at the waist. Everything worn, nothing
new, nothing decorative. Boots built for standing in water.

Include a small secondary detail view in a corner: the lens rig seen closer, and
the notebook and pencil.

Neutral even lighting so colour and construction read clearly. Do NOT stylise
the lighting on a reference sheet.

Square, 1:1. No text, no labels, no captions, no watermark, no border, no frame.
Full bleed to all four edges.
```

## 2 — BLOCK 44, STREET LEVEL

Save as `references/LOCATIONS/BLOCK_44_SURFACE/loc-block-44-surface.png`

```
Location canonical, CLIMBERS pulp sci-fi illustration style. 3:2 LANDSCAPE.
PALETTE MODE: PS-1 NIGHT NOIR.

BLOCK 44, STREET LEVEL — an ordinary Haven City residential street, older than
the Tower district, seen during a glitch stutter.

Establishing view down the length of the block, camera at street level. Show
construction and layout, not drama.

ARCHITECTURE: older, heavier construction than the Tower district. Blockwork
frontages, exposed conduit runs strapped along the walls, service hatches at
pavement level, external stair runs. Functional municipal building, four to six
storeys, no ornament. This is a working neighbourhood, NOT a slum and NOT a ruin
— maintained, occupied, unglamorous.

THE STUTTER, which is the state this location is always seen in: STREET
LIGHTING AT HALF POWER and UNEVEN. Some fixtures dim, some out entirely, light
pooling under a few and failing between them. Do not light this street evenly.

THE OPEN DOORS: doors along both sides WEDGED OPEN with whatever came to hand —
a brick, a crate, a folded rag — and windows CRACKED open. Warm interior light
leaks from every one of those gaps, and that leaked light is the only warm light
in the scene. The whole block has done the same thing at the same time without
being told to.

AT ONE END: the WATER RECLAMATION BUILDING. Squat, industrial, tanks and vent
stacks, plainly a piece of infrastructure rather than a home. Behind it, an
ACCESS SHAFT head — a plain municipal hatch set in the ground, CLOSED,
unremarkable, not lit and not emphasised. It must be present and easy to miss.

NO CHARACTERS. If scale is needed, one anonymous unlit silhouette at a great
distance, no face and no costume identity.

Deep navy and near-black structure, cold dim cyan-white from the failing street
fixtures, warm amber leaking from the propped doors. No fog, no rain, no ruin,
no smoke.

3:2 landscape. No text, no labels, no captions, no watermark, no border, no
frame. Full bleed to all four edges.
```

## 3 — BLOCK 44 SUB-LEVEL SERVICE GALLERY

Save as `references/LOCATIONS/SUB_LEVEL_GALLERY/loc-sub-level-gallery.png`

```
Location canonical, CLIMBERS pulp sci-fi illustration style. 3:2 LANDSCAPE.
PALETTE MODE: PS-1 NIGHT NOIR.

BLOCK 44 SUB-LEVEL SERVICE GALLERY — the maintenance space under the block.

Establishing view along the length of the gallery, camera low, near the water.

CONSTRUCTION: long, low, PRE-SERAPH — visibly older, heavier and cruder than
anything on the surface. Poured foundation and heavy blockwork, low ceiling with
structural ribs, everything built for access rather than for people.

PIPE AND CONDUIT run along BOTH walls in banked municipal runs — water mains,
power conduit, junction collars, valve stations, bracket fixings bleeding rust.
Every run is a tagged, numbered, maintained municipal installation.

STANDING WATER covers the floor, a couple of inches deep, still enough to hold
clean reflections of the pipework above it. The water is the floor of every shot
taken here.

LIGHT: there is NO fixed lighting in this gallery. The only light is what a
worker brings in — show it as a single hard lamp cone from the camera side,
raking down the length of the space. THE CONE ENDS IN BLACK. The far end of the
gallery is not dim, it is absent: the light simply stops.

AT THE NORTH END, both of which must be present and legible in this reference:
- A heavy UNMARKED FEEDER PIPE entering the gallery through what reads as
  unbroken foundation stone. No frame, no collar, no opening around it. It comes
  out of solid wall. It is plainer and older than the municipal runs.
- Four meters east of it, the painted-over OUTLINE OF A MAINTENANCE DOOR: a
  rectangular seam under sixty years of layered municipal paint, hinges painted
  over into lumps rather than hardware. It is a SHAPE in the wall, not an
  object. It is SHUT and NO light escapes around it.

NO CHARACTERS AT ALL in this reference.

Near-black and deep navy stone, wet reflective floor, dead municipal green-grey
paint on the north wall, one hard warm-white lamp cone. Almost no saturation.

3:2 landscape. No text, no labels, no captions, no watermark, no border, no
frame. Full bleed to all four edges.
```

## 4 — THE BLOCK THAT FELL OFF THE MAP

Save as `references/LOCATIONS/THE_LOST_BLOCK/loc-the-lost-block.png`

```
Location canonical, CLIMBERS pulp sci-fi illustration style. 3:2 LANDSCAPE.
PALETTE MODE: PS-2 SUNSET EMBER, read as LAMPLIGHT rather than sky.

THE LOST BLOCK — an entire sub-level street the city has no record of, lived in
by thirty people.

Establishing view down the length of the street, camera at standing height,
low ceiling overhead.

THE ONE THING TO GET RIGHT: THIS IS A HOME. It is maintained, lit, planted,
swept and lived in, and it is WARMER AND MORE HUMAN THAN ANYTHING ON THE
SURFACE. It is NOT a slum, NOT a hideout, NOT a bunker, NOT a ruin, NOT a
shanty, NOT post-apocalyptic. Nobody here is desperate. Somebody sweeps this
street. If it looks like squalor the reference has failed.

CONSTRUCTION: an old sub-level block from before a rebuild nobody remembers.
Low ceiling, heavy SUPPORT COLUMNS at intervals, paving stones underfoot, solid
old blockwork frontages either side.

WHAT IS IN IT, all of which must be present:
- STRINGS OF SMALL WHITE LAMPS running the length of the street between the
  columns, wired clean and spliced well. These are the main light source. They
  are STRUNG, not installed — visible cable, neat work.
- LAUNDRY on lines between the support columns, catching the lamplight.
- A GARDEN of pale vegetables under GROW LAMPS, planted in raised beds built out
  of lifted paving stones. The grow lamps are cool green-white and are the only
  cold light in the scene.
- A CISTERN with a valve rig on it — visibly GOOD work, better than municipal,
  clean welds and proper fittings.
- TWENTY TO THIRTY SMALL DWELLINGS along both sides with CURTAINS FOR DOORS,
  each with its own light behind the curtain.
- A child's CHALK DRAWING looping across the paving stones.
- Steam or cooking smoke somewhere in the middle distance.

NO CHARACTERS in this reference. The street is empty; the plates will populate
it.

Warm amber and soft white from the strung lamps, cool green-white from the grow
lamps as the single cold accent, deep navy structure holding it together. Warm
dominant.

3:2 landscape. No text, no labels, no captions, no watermark, no border, no
frame. Full bleed to all four edges.
```

## 5 — THE WHITE ROOM

Save as `references/LOCATIONS/WHITE_ROOM/loc-white-room.png`

```
Location canonical, CLIMBERS pulp sci-fi illustration style. 3:2 LANDSCAPE.
PALETTE MODE: NONE. This location deliberately breaks the palette system. It is
white, near-white and pale grey. Do not warm it, do not cool it, do not grade it.

THE WHITE ROOM — where memories are taken. It is frightening because it is
gentle, and every instruction below serves that.

Establishing view of the room, camera slightly high, taking in the walls, the
ceiling and the table.

THE DEFINING PROPERTY, AND THE HARDEST: THERE ARE NO SHADOWS IN THIS ROOM.
Light arrives from every direction at once. NOTHING casts a shadow — not the
table, not its legs, not a wall fitting, nothing. There is no falloff into any
corner, no gradient down any wall, no darkening where surfaces meet, no ambient
occlusion, no contact shadow anywhere. Form is carried by line and the faintest
tonal shift only. The room should look subtly impossible and a viewer should
not immediately be able to say why. Do NOT add a soft shadow "to help it read" —
the absence IS the reference.

SURFACES: WHITE TILE walls, small-format, clean grout, immaculately kept. A
WHITE CEILING that is itself luminous, edge to edge, with no visible fixture,
no panel seams, no light housing. White floor.

AT THE CENTRE: a single TABLE. It is COMFORTABLE — padded, upholstered, clean,
considerate, the sort of surface built for somebody's ease rather than for a
procedure. This detail is the worst thing in the room and it must be
unmistakable.

WHAT IS NOT IN THIS ROOM: no restraints, no straps, no clamps, no instruments,
no trays, no tools, no monitors, no wires, no drains, no stains, no blood, no
medical clutter of any kind. No cameras. No signage. Nothing menacing in any
prop. The room is EMPTY, CLEAN AND KIND-LOOKING, and that is what makes it
terrible.

NO CHARACTERS in this reference.

White, near-white, the palest grey, and nothing else. The lowest-contrast image
in the entire project, deliberately.

3:2 landscape. No text, no labels, no captions, no watermark, no border, no
frame. Full bleed to all four edges.
```

### Part One: The Night Before

Rainbow had run The Prism for nineteen years and he knew, within thirty seconds of someone walking through the door, exactly what kind of trouble they were.

He was not a small man and The Prism was not a small bar, but the hat made him impossible to miss regardless. Wide-brimmed, spiked crown, nineteen years of patches and pins and small tokens from people who had needed somewhere to put something and chosen his hat as the place. His coat was every color at once, layered and heavy, built up over the same nineteen years the way reefs were built, one piece at a time. He wore the brim low, and in nineteen years nobody had got a proper look at his face. What you got was the eye. His left eye was his own and you rarely saw it. His right eye had belonged to a Bone Patrol officer until Absalom decided Rainbow needed a reminder about the cost of harboring Unbound, and it sat in that shadow burning a low steady red. He had kept it because it was better than the alternative, and because he had decided a long time ago not to hide what the city had done to him. So he hid the rest. The cane was for posture. He'd been told that. He didn't entirely believe it.

The Prism sat at the end of the approach road to the Tower gate, which meant everyone who registered in the morning walked past it, and most of them found their way inside the night before. Rainbow had learned to read registration eve the way a sailor read weather. By the time both teams had come and gone he'd have a picture of each of them clear enough to last.

Sable was already at the bar when the first of them arrived. He usually was, the night before a registration. He sat where he always sat, third stool from the wall, a lean man with a face you'd forget and a memory that had never lost anything, nursing the one drink he would nurse all night. Sable had climbed, years back. He'd come down again with his answer and with the small strange gift the Tower gave the ones it answered and released: he could not lose a path he had walked once. Not in the dark, not blind, not after the Tower rearranged itself. It made him the best Sherpa money could contract, and it made Vesper Quill the holder of his contract, and it made him, on nights like this, a man who watched the door and kept his own quiet count of who came through it. He was one of the touched, the ones the city called the Ascended, and like most of them he wore it quietly. You had to know what he was to see it.

"Busy tomorrow?" Rainbow asked him, early, before the teams came in.

"Two teams, one morning." Sable turned his glass a quarter turn. "Somebody's going to want eyes on that."

Marcus's team came in together, by the front door, a few minutes after the tenth bell.

The Human man with the military cybernetic walked point. Marcus Torres, Rainbow would learn before the night was out, when Marcus paid his tab and signed the chit with the flat economy of a man who had been filling out official forms his whole life. The eye behind Marcus's left socket caught the light wrong when he turned his head, a faint blue-white gleam that tracked the room in pieces before Marcus himself finished looking around. Broad-shouldered, military posture that had calcified into something he probably couldn't turn off. He sat at the bar two stools from the end, ordered something plain, and watched the door.

The one who came in behind him had to duck at the frame. Signal was what the team called him, a name Rainbow overheard from the woman with the green cuffs when she flagged the bar for another round later in the evening. He was a Korinth combat chassis, black and grey composite, built for function at the cost of subtlety, and he moved through The Prism with the careful deliberateness of something very large trying not to break the furniture. He took a table near the back and folded his hands on the surface and sat. The chassis was Korinth manufacture, a model Rainbow recognized from the retrieval notices that cycled through the city's commercial feeds. KI series. There had been a bounty flag on that particular model for going on three months. Korinth wanted it back. The notice hadn't said why and notices like that never did, but the reward number had been large enough that Rainbow had remembered it.

The Valari woman came in last and took the corner booth. Nyx'ara was the name Marcus used when he brought her a second drink without being asked, the small courtesy of teammates who had been at this long enough to know each other's habits. She was tall even sitting, circuit patterns running the length of her neck and jaw, drifting slow between a soft blue and a dusty magenta-violet the way Valari circuits did when their owner was holding still on purpose. She kept her hands flat on the table. She looked up every time the door opened. She had ordered something mild and had not touched it in forty minutes.

Rainbow watched her watch the door and understood, in the way he understood most things about people who came to The Prism the night before a registration, that she was not waiting for anyone in particular. She was waiting for the feeling to pass. It wasn't going to pass.

Kimo's team came in by the side entrance, separately, within ten minutes of each other.

The Human woman in green and gold arrived first, a mechanical arm on her right side that had seen recent work, fresh tool marks at the elbow joint, integrated deep enough that the shoulder sat differently than the left. She introduced herself to Signal's table on the way past, a quick handshake and the name Spark, and Rainbow noted that she shook with the mechanical hand by habit rather than courtesy, which meant she had stopped thinking of it as the unusual one. She went to the bar, ordered something complicated, watched Rainbow make it with the focused attention of someone who finds everything interesting, and then carried it to Signal's table and sat down and started talking. She had the easy energy of someone whose mind was already made up.

Kimo Tomiko came in five minutes after Spark. His face was fully concealed behind a dark mask, a single eye burning at the left socket in a specific orange-gold that Rainbow had never seen on a Human augmentation before. He moved through the room with the careful geometric efficiency of someone who had counted exits before sitting down, which was a habit Rainbow recognized because it was his own. He sat at the bar, ordered nothing, and then changed his mind and ordered something small, and Rainbow brought it without comment.

The last one through the side door was the Valari. He took a stool at the far end of the bar and set his hands on the surface and said nothing. He wore a demon mask over his Valari features, the circuits beneath it hidden down to almost nothing. Not dimmed the way Nyx'ara's were dim, suppressed by the city's field and her own effort. Hidden. Chosen. Rainbow had seen Valari dim their circuits out of grief or habit or fear of the Bone Patrol, but he had never seen a Valari work this hard at being invisible inside his own biology.

Rainbow had heard Kimo call him Tal'Voss once, quietly, at the end of the bar, a half-sentence about the morning's timing. He had the traditional Valari name, the carried-lineage name, three letters and an apostrophe and four more, the kind of name that arrived with a person's ancestry attached. He was using it. For now.

Rainbow set a glass in front of him without asking. Tal'Voss didn't touch it for ten minutes, and then he drank it in two swallows, and Rainbow refilled it without comment.

The two Valari never looked directly at each other. The room wasn't large enough for that to be accidental. At some point in the middle of the evening, when Tal'Voss shifted on his stool and his masked face turned a few degrees toward the corner booth, Nyx'ara's circuit patterns did something at her jawline, a flicker of violet through the blue, quick and suppressed, like a word she had decided not to say. Tal'Voss's circuits didn't respond. He had felt it. He had chosen not to.

Rainbow filed that behind the bar with the other things that were going to matter later.

Sable left an hour before closing, unhurried, his one drink finally finished. He stopped at the door and looked back at the room, at both teams, the way a man looks at a route he expects to walk again.

At closing time all six of them left by different doors into the same night, toward the same morning. Rainbow looked at the six empty glasses and the one glass still full. He poured himself two fingers of the Valari grain spirit he kept under the bar for occasions that earned it. He did not drink it for a while. Then he did.

Whatever happened tomorrow, he would be there to watch. He always was.

### Part Two: The Oath of Ascent

The Tower ground plaza held three hundred people on a quiet week.

This was not a quiet week.

Rainbow stood near the back of the crowd in his coat and his hat and watched Haven City compress itself into the space before the registration wall. Bone Patrol on the perimeter, four of them, chrome skull-faces and red optics and hammers loose at their sides. Thorne's people somewhere in the crowd, invisible the way Thorne's people were always invisible, present only as the quality of attention that shifted when you moved near them. On every building face in the plaza the screens had already switched to the registration feed. You couldn't escape the Oath of Ascent in Haven City if you tried. Seraph made sure of that. The questions belonged to the city the moment they left a climber's mouth.

Unit-9911 stood at the front of the ceremony in its dark hooded robe, hands pressed together at the chest, the single ring of pale light at its forehead the only bright thing about it. It had been presiding over the Oath of Ascent for as long as Rainbow could remember, and it carried that history in its posture, a stillness that was not indifference. It had watched hundreds of people speak their questions into stone, and it stood like something that remembered all of them.
